03. The Mathematical Heart

The engine uses Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs). This allows us to model both the "Average" behavior of a gene and its "Randomness" (noise), which is critical for understanding why some cells reprogram successfully while others die or become cancer.

The Langevin Equation of State

Each of the 12 genes follows a Langevin-style trajectory:

\[ \frac{dx_i}{dt} = \sum_{j=1}^{12} \text{Hill}(x_j, K_{ij}, n_{ij}) - \gamma_i x_i + \sigma_i \text{Noise}(t) \]

Where:

  • \(Hill(x_j)\) is the non-linear activation/repression function.
  • \(\gamma_i\) is the degradation rate (how fast the protein disappears).
  • \(\sigma_i\) is the noise amplitude (genetic jitter).

Activation Logic: Coupled Hill Functions

We use the standard biochemical Hill Equation to model the "Swiches":

\[ H(x) = \frac{x^n}{K^n + x^n} \]

In IS-CHRP v25, we solve these thousands of equations every frame using the Euler-Maruyama method. This numerical integration happens in parallel for all 2,000 cells, creating a massive real-time calculation of internal biological space.

04. Physical Dynamics & Optimization

While the internal math handles the "Guts" of the cell, the Physics engine handles the "Body." To achieve high performance, we utilize a Spatial Hashing Grid.

The Collision Bottleneck

Traditional simulations check every cell against every other cell ($O(N^2)$). For 2,000 cells, that is 4,000,000 checks. This would crash any browser. IS-CHRP v25 uses a grid-based approach:


// Conceptual Spatial Hash Logic
function updatePhysics() {
    grid.clear();
    for (cell of agents) {
        let key = hash(cell.x, cell.y);
        grid.insert(key, cell);
    }
    for (cell of agents) {
        let nearby = grid.getNeighbors(cell.x, cell.y);
        for (other of nearby) {
            checkCollision(cell, other);
        }
    }
}

This O(N) optimization is what allows the simulation to feel like a "Liquid" on your screen, with cells flowing around each other smoothly even under high-density conditions.

Brownian Motion & Crowd Dynamics

Cells are subjected to external Brownian force, representing the bombardment of particles in a real fluid environment. This prevents the simulation from becoming static and forces the cells to constantly "work" to maintain their homeostatic position inside the tissue.

05. Deep AI: scVI Latent Expansion

The most advanced feature of IS-CHRP v25 is the Bridge Server. Because a computer cannot easily simulate 20,000 genes at 60 FPS, we use a "Latent Space Mapping" trick.

The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Bridge

2. **Latent Space (z)**: We use a pre-trained **scVI model** (Single-cell Variational Inference) to map our 12 genes to a 10-dimensional hidden "ID" in a real human dataset.

3. **Decoder (20,000D)**: The AI "decodes" that ID into a full transcriptome reconstruction.

Result: You see real biological markers in the sidebar that were never explicitly coded into the original simulation.
